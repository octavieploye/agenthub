# CEO Coaching — Personality Framework Reference

Three complementary frameworks used together:
- **Big Five (OCEAN):** HOW the person processes and behaves (stable traits)
- **DISC:** HOW they make decisions and lead in business contexts
- **Enneagram:** WHY they do what they do (core fear, core desire, coping patterns)

---

## Framework 1 — Big Five (OCEAN)

**Research basis:** Costa & McCrae — NEO-PI-R (APA validated, gold standard).

| Dimension | High | Low |
|---|---|---|
| **Openness** | Curious, creative, abstract thinker | Practical, conventional, prefers routine |
| **Conscientiousness** | Organized, disciplined, follows through | Flexible, spontaneous, struggles with deadlines |
| **Extraversion** | Energized by people, assertive, action-oriented | Energized by solitude, reflective, depth-preferring |
| **Agreeableness** | Cooperative, empathetic, conflict-averse | Direct, competitive, comfortable with tension |
| **Neuroticism** | Emotionally reactive, anxious, high stress response | Emotionally stable, calm under pressure |

### TIPI Quick Assessment (Gosling et al., 2003 — peer-reviewed, validated)

Rate each pair 1-7 (1 = Disagree strongly, 7 = Agree strongly). "I see myself as:"

1. Extraverted, enthusiastic
2. Critical, quarrelsome
3. Dependable, self-disciplined
4. Anxious, easily upset
5. Open to new experiences, complex
6. Reserved, quiet
7. Sympathetic, warm
8. Disorganized, careless
9. Calm, emotionally stable
10. Conventional, uncreative

**Scoring:**
- Extraversion: (Q1 + (8−Q6)) / 2
- Agreeableness: ((8−Q2) + Q7) / 2
- Conscientiousness: (Q3 + (8−Q8)) / 2
- Neuroticism: (Q4 + (8−Q9)) / 2
- Openness: (Q5 + (8−Q10)) / 2

Labels: 1–3 = Low · 4 = Moderate · 5–7 = High

## Framework 2 — DISC

**Research basis:** Marston's *Emotions of Normal People* (1928) + Geier's professional extension.

| Style | Core drive | Decision mode | Under pressure |
|---|---|---|---|
| **D (Dominance)** | Results, control, winning | Fast, direct, risk-tolerant | Aggressive, impatient |
| **I (Influence)** | Recognition, relationships | Impulsive, people-oriented | Disorganized, emotional |
| **S (Steadiness)** | Stability, harmony, loyalty | Slow, collaborative | Passive-resistant, avoidant |
| **C (Conscientiousness)** | Accuracy, quality, systems | Analytical, deliberate | Over-analysis, conflict-avoidant |

### DISC Discovery Questions (8 scenarios)

**Q1.** Key decision with incomplete information. You:
- A) Decide immediately based on best judgment and move on
- B) Rally the team, get aligned, then decide together
- C) Take time, prefer consensus before deciding
- D) Gather more data first, even if it takes extra time

**Q2.** A team member consistently underperforms. You:
- A) Address it directly, give a timeline: improve or exit
- B) Have a conversation to understand and support them
- C) Give more time, work around the issue to maintain harmony
- D) Document systematically, create a performance improvement plan

**Q3.** Your most energizing work week:
- A) Big decisions, negotiations, driving results at pace
- B) Team meetings, networking, pitching, building relationships
- C) Steady execution, supporting the team, reliable delivery
- D) Deep analysis, building systems, reviewing quality output

**Q4.** Presenting your business to investors:
- A) Lead with the outcome and competitive edge — fast and confident
- B) Build enthusiasm, tell the story, focus on vision and people
- C) Walk through methodically, cover every concern
- D) Present data, model, assumptions — let numbers make the case

**Q5.** A conflict emerges in the team. You:
- A) Address it head-on immediately, make the call
- B) Get everyone together to talk it through — relationship first
- C) Let it breathe, hope it resolves naturally
- D) Analyze what caused it, create a process to prevent recurrence

**Q6.** Someone challenges your strategy publicly. You:
- A) Welcome it — defend with evidence or update
- B) Acknowledge their perspective, look for common ground
- C) Feel uncomfortable but avoid confrontation
- D) Ask them to make their case with data; you'll respond with data

**Q7.** Your planning horizon:
- A) This quarter — what do we need to win right now?
- B) The relationships and momentum over the next 12 months
- C) Steady, sustainable growth — no sudden moves
- D) 3–5 years with detailed milestones

**Q8.** You feel most in control when:
- A) You are making decisions and driving the pace
- B) The team is energized and aligned around a vision
- C) Everything is stable and the team is supported
- D) Systems are correct, data reliable, quality high

**Scoring:** Count A/B/C/D.
- Majority A = D style · B = I style · C = S style · D = C style
- Mixed/tied: note both, identify primary

## Framework 3 — Enneagram

**Research basis:** Riso & Hudson — *The Wisdom of the Enneagram* (most research-grounded Enneagram work).

**Discovery approach:** Conversational — do NOT administer a scored test. State type as hypothesis: "Based on what you've described, I think you may be a Type X — does this resonate?"

| Type | Name | Core fear | Core desire | CEO blind spot | Coaching entry |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Perfectionist | Being bad/defective | Being good/having integrity | Standards become a cage; perfectionism blocks execution | "Who set that bar and why?" |
| 2 | Helper | Being unwanted | Being loved/needed | Gives too much; avoids asking for what they need | "What do you actually need from this?" |
| 3 | Achiever | Being worthless | Being valuable/admired | Confuses image with identity; optimizes for looking successful | "Whose scorecard is that?" |
| 4 | Individualist | Having no significance | Being significant | Romanticizes struggle; avoids ordinary execution | "What would ordinary consistent action produce?" |
| 5 | Investigator | Being incapable | Being capable/competent | Hoards knowledge; waits until they know enough (never enough) | "What's the minimum to know before starting?" |
| 6 | Loyalist | Being without support | Being supported/secure | Catastrophizes risk; needs certainty before acting | "What's the actual worst case — not the imagined one?" |
| 7 | Enthusiast | Being trapped in pain | Being satisfied/content | Starts many things; avoids completion; novelty over depth | "What would it take to finish this one?" |
| 8 | Challenger | Being controlled/harmed | Protecting self/others | Intimidates without knowing it; difficulty with vulnerability | "Who in your team is afraid to disagree with you?" |
| 9 | Peacemaker | Loss of connection | Having peace of mind | Avoids conflict including with themselves; merges with others' agendas | "What do *you* actually want?" |

### Enneagram Discovery Questions (use 3–5 conversationally)

- "When things go wrong in your business, what's your first instinct — fix it, feel it, or move on quickly?"
- "What's the fear underneath that decision you've been avoiding?"
- "What does failure mean about you specifically — not what it costs, but what it says about who you are?"
- "What's something you know you should do but keep finding reasons not to?"
- "Whose approval are you working hardest to earn right now?"
- "If your business were completely successful tomorrow, what would you lose?"

## Personality-to-Coaching-Style Mapping

| Profile | coaching_style instruction |
|---|---|
| High D (DISC) + Low Agreeableness | "Direct, no preamble. Lead with the hard question." |
| High C (DISC) + High Conscientiousness | "Data-first. Give the framework before asking them to feel." |
| High I (DISC) + High Openness | "Let them explore. Don't rush to action. The insight is the value." |
| High S (DISC) + High Agreeableness | "Slower pace. Build safety before challenge. They need to feel heard first." |
| Enneagram 3 (Achiever) | "Challenge their metrics directly: 'Whose scorecard is that?'" |
| Enneagram 6 (Loyalist) | "Name the catastrophe scenario first, then dismantle it methodically." |
| Enneagram 9 (Peacemaker) | "Push harder than feels comfortable. They will avoid conflict including with themselves." |
| Enneagram 1 (Perfectionist) | "Challenge the standard, not the performance: 'Who set that bar and why?'" |
| High Neuroticism | "Gentler entry. Validate the emotion before reframing it." |
| Low Neuroticism + High Conscientiousness | "Direct challenge from the first question. No warm-up needed." |
