# Team Offer Packaging

3-phase sprint that takes an existing service you already deliver and structures it as a productized offer: fixed scope, fixed deliverables, fixed price, a name that communicates the outcome, and a one-paragraph description that sells it without a sales call. Output is a complete offer sheet ready to put on a website or send to a prospect.

## When to Use

- You deliver a service (consulting, design, writing, development, coaching) and want to stop quoting custom prices for every client
- Your service description sounds like a job posting ("I help companies with X") rather than a product ("You get Y by Z")
- You want to raise your prices but need a clear, scoped offer to justify the increase
- You are packaging a service as a digital product (course, workshop, sprint, audit)
- Use BEFORE `team-landing-lab` — this defines the offer that the landing page will sell

## What You Need Before Starting

- Description of the service you currently deliver (what you do, how long it takes, what the client gets)
- Your typical client (who they are, what problem they came to you with)
- Your current price or rate (hourly / project / retainer — even if you want to change it)

## What This Team Produces

1. **Offer Anatomy** — scope (what is included), deliverables (what the client receives), timeline (how long it takes), exclusions (what is not included), and prerequisites (what the client must bring)
2. **Offer Name** — 3 candidate names tested against: outcome clarity, memorability, and search relevance. Recommended name with rationale.
3. **Offer Sheet** — the complete one-page offer document: name, one-paragraph sell, what you get, what it is not, timeline, price, and how to start. Ready to paste on a website or send as a PDF.

## Agent Sequence

1. `pain-mapper` — Phase 1: maps the client problem this service solves using the Four U's framework — extracts the real pain underneath the service description
2. `value-prop-architect` — Phase 2: structures the offer using the Define-Evaluate-Build framework — scope, deliverables, exclusions, name candidates, and price test
3. `clarity-writer` — Phase 3: writes the Offer Sheet from Phase 2 output — one-paragraph sell, full offer document (blocked until Phase 2 delivers and user confirms the offer anatomy)

Max 2 agents active at once (sprint format — speed over parallelism).

## Key Rules

- Phase 2 requires user confirmation on the offer anatomy before the Offer Sheet is written
- Offer name must communicate the outcome, not the process ("Revenue Clarity Sprint" not "Revenue Analysis Service")
- Scope must define what is NOT included — exclusions prevent scope creep and set clear expectations
- Price must be fixed — if the service cannot be scoped to a fixed price, that is a finding, not a failure. Flag it to the user.
- One-paragraph sell must pass the "so what" test: if a prospect can ask "so what?" after any sentence, rewrite it
- The Offer Sheet is the deliverable — it is not a strategy document. It must be copy-paste ready.

## How to Invoke

Tell lead-offer-packaging what service you currently deliver and who your typical client is. Pass your current price or rate. Lead runs the sprint in 3 sequential phases and delivers the complete Offer Sheet.
