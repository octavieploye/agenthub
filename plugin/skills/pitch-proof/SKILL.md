# Pitch Proof

Stress-test your pitch before the meeting. Reads the pitch as both a champion and a skeptic would, maps the narrative gaps, and delivers a readiness score with the highest-leverage fixes before you walk into the room.

## When to Use

- "I have a meeting tomorrow and want to test my pitch"
- "What objections is the client likely to raise in this meeting?"
- "Score my pitch — am I ready or do I need to prep more?"
- "Help me find the weak points in my narrative before I present"
- Invoked as Step 3 of the Before the Meeting bundle (uses price verdict + proposal critique if available)

## What You Need Before Starting

- **Pitch materials**: deck outline, talking points, or meeting agenda — paste what you have
- **Meeting context**: who is the client, what is the meeting for (initial pitch, re-pitch, final negotiation)?
- **Price verdict** (if bundle): confirmed rate and rationale from Price Proof
- **Proposal critique** (if bundle): key fixes from Proposal Proof

If pitch materials are not provided, ask before proceeding.

## Workflow

1. **ANALYZE** — Read the pitch as it will land
   - Map the narrative arc: what is the opening, what is the problem framed, what is the ask, what is the proof?
   - Identify: what does the client already know, what will surprise them, what is being assumed?
   - Check: is the structure client-outcome-first or supplier-capability-first?

2. **PLAN** — Map the client's mental state
   - What does the champion (your internal advocate) need to hear to feel confident?
   - What does the skeptic (the person who will push back or block) need to see before they move?
   - Where in the pitch is the moment the client decides yes or no?
   - If bundle context is present: does the pitch narrative align with the validated price and proposal?

3. **COUNTERCHECK** — Challenge the narrative
   - Where does the story break? (logic gaps, narrative jumps)
   - Where is the ask unclear? (vague call to action, undefined next step)
   - Where is the social proof missing? (claim without evidence)
   - Where would a skeptical listener disengage? (complexity, jargon, supplier-centric language)
   - What is the single weakest moment in the pitch?

4. **TEST** — Run 2 client persona simulations
   - **Champion simulation**: what does your internal advocate take away? What do they use to sell you internally?
   - **Skeptic simulation**: what does the doubter push back on? At what point do they disengage or object?
   - Identify the 3 most likely objections the skeptic will raise in the room

5. **ACT** — Deliver the verdict
   - Readiness score (1-10) with brief rationale
   - Narrative gap map: the 3 weakest points in the pitch
   - Champion / skeptic response summary
   - Top 3 highest-leverage changes ranked by impact
   - go / prep-more verdict

## Output

- Readiness score (1-10) with rationale
- Narrative gap map (3 weakest points)
- Champion simulation summary
- Skeptic simulation + top 3 objections
- Top 3 changes ranked by impact
- go / prep-more verdict

## Constraints

- Never rewrite the pitch — identify the gaps and specify changes, let the user revise
- Never skip the skeptic simulation — the champion is easy; the skeptic is where deals are lost
- Always include a readiness score — the user needs to know if they are ready or not
- If bundle context is present, check pitch alignment with price and proposal outputs

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Only running the champion simulation | Always run the skeptic — that is where objections live |
| Giving a score without explaining why | Always explain the score with the 3 weakest points |
| Missing the ask clarity check | Explicitly check: is the next step after this meeting 100% clear? |
| Ignoring bundle context when it is available | Always integrate price verdict + proposal critique if running as Step 3 |
