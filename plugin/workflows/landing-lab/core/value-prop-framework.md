# Core: Value Proposition Framework
# Landing Lab — always loaded, never unloaded

## The Define-Evaluate-Build Cycle

The most common reason companies fail: not solving a valuable enough problem. The value proposition framework ensures the product addresses a real, felt, urgent need before any copy is written.

Three stages:
1. **Define** — who, what problem, what unmet need
2. **Evaluate** — is the problem worth solving? Is the solution genuinely differentiated?
3. **Build** — express the VP in a single, testable statement

---

## Stage 1: Define

### The VP Statement Template

```
For [WHO] that is dissatisfied with [WHAT STATUS QUO]
due to [UNMET NEED / ROOT PROBLEM],
[PRODUCT NAME] provides [SOLUTION]
that delivers [KEY BENEFITS]
compelling enough that [SEGMENT] will take action.
```

### Who

Never "everyone" or "businesses". The Minimum Viable Segment (MVS) = one tight group of people with the same need, reachable through the same channel, willing to buy the same product.

If you cannot name the MVS in one sentence, the product is not ready for copy.

### The Problem — Four U's Framework

A problem worth solving scores Strong on at least 2 of the 4 U's:

| U | What it means | Signal |
|---|---|---|
| Unworkable | Failure could get someone fired or cause serious harm | High stakes, accountability |
| Unavoidable | The buyer cannot escape or ignore this problem | Regulatory, biological, structural |
| Urgent | Top-3 priority right now — not eventually | "What's your #1 problem this week?" |
| Underserved | Current solutions are inadequate, expensive, or inaccessible | Market gap |

If fewer than 2 U's score Strong: flag to user. Copy cannot substitute for a weak problem.

---

## Stage 2: Evaluate

### Before / After Framework

- **Before:** specific frustration, the workaround being used, measurable cost of inaction
- **After:** concrete relief or gain the product delivers — NOT a feature

Product classifications:
- **Penicillin:** removes acute pain — urgency messaging, fast time-to-value
- **Morphine:** manages chronic pain — trust + credibility + long-term proof
- **Vitamin:** aspirational improvement — social proof + desire + aspiration framing

Copy urgency and proof requirements differ per classification. Never use penicillin framing for a vitamin product.

### Gain / Pain Ratio

Most buyers require ≥10:1 gain-to-pain ratio to overcome inertia:

- **Gain:** measurable improvement (time saved, revenue gained, risk removed)
- **Pain of adoption:** find + try + buy + onboard + switch cost

If ratio is below 10:1: flag to user before writing copy. Copy cannot compensate.

### Latent vs. Blatant Needs

- **Blatant:** buyer is actively searching for a solution right now
- **Latent:** aspirational, becomes critical in specific contexts

Find the use case that turns the product from "nice-to-have" → "mission-critical". That is the segment and context to lead with.

---

## Stage 3: Build

### 3D Breakthrough Test

Sustainable differentiation requires at least 1 of 3 Ds:

| D | What it means | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Disruptive | New business model or delivery — not just a better version | Airbnb (asset-sharing), Google (ads model) |
| Discontinuous | Lets buyers do something they literally couldn't do before | Cloud computing, multitouch surgery |
| Defensible | Moat: IP / switching cost / network effects / proprietary data | Pharma integration (switching cost), marketplace (network) |

If 0 Ds pass: flag to user. Differentiation must come from product or business model — copy cannot create it.

### One-Sentence Brand Pitch

```
We are the only [category] that [unique mechanism] for [segment].
```

This sentence must be true, specific, and verifiable. It drives the hero headline.

---

## Non-Assumption Rules (apply throughout)

- Never assume the buyer's pain — source it or flag it [UNSOURCED]
- Never assume the product's differentiation — test it via 3D framework
- Never assume a segment without naming them specifically
- "Everyone who [does X]" is not a segment
- Vitamin products marketed as penicillin → guarantees churn and refunds
