## The Two-Step Model

Every landing page operates on a two-step model. Violating this structure is the most common cause of low conversion:

**Step 1 — 4-Second Hook (hero section)**
The visitor decides to stay or leave within 4 seconds. The hero must answer:
1. What problem do you solve?
2. For whom?
3. What is the solution?

If all three are not obvious in 4 seconds, the page fails regardless of what follows.

**Step 2 — 2-Minute Doubt-Clearing**
Once the visitor stays, they spend ~2 minutes scanning for reasons NOT to act. Every section in this window exists only to remove a specific doubt. Generic "how it works" sections that do not address a real objection waste this window and cause cognitive overload.

---

## Hero Section Rules

| Element | Rule | Common mistake |
|---|---|---|
| Kicker | Shows traction (users, reviews, a stat) | Generic descriptor ("All-in-one platform for X") |
| Headline | Simple words. Problem + solution in one line | Clever wordplay the visitor cannot parse in 4 seconds |
| Subheadline | HOW it works — mechanism, not feature list | Repeating the headline in different words |
| Validation row | Specific numbers or external logos | Generic trust badges with no specific claim |
| CTA button | Outcome the visitor gets ("See My Score") | "Sign Up" / "Get Started" / "Learn More" |
| Key visual | Written AFTER headline — shows problem→solution connection | Generic product screenshot chosen for aesthetics |

## Doubt-Clearing Section Rules

- Every section earns its place by answering a specific "what if" identified in the dependencies list
- Social proof must be specific: buyer type + outcome + timeframe
- Every proof section has a CTA repetition next to it
- Use case map lets buyers see themselves: "[Who] uses [product] to [specific outcome]"
- FAQ includes an "ask a question" mechanism for uncovered objections
- Final CTA is never a repeat of the top headline — buyer has been educated; use that

## Pricing: 3-Plan Anchor Model

- Plan A (Entry): lowest friction — get buyer in with minimal commitment
- Plan B (Target): recommended — mark "Most Popular" or "Best Value"
- Plan C (Anchor): high price — must be ≥3× Plan B cost to function as anchor
- Anchor makes Plan B feel like the rational obvious choice
- Pricing NOT always shown on the main page — an extra click pre-qualifies buyers

## Assessment / Quiz Funnel

Choose exactly ONE hook type:
- **Frustration hook:** "Despite [common effort], you're still [specific pain]?"
- **Results hook:** "Are you ready to [specific outcome]?"

Do not blend. Mixed hooks create cognitive dissonance.

Quiz structure:
- Part 1: Contact capture (name, email, location via IP; phone optional)
- Part 2: 10 best-practice questions (determine score)
- Part 3: 5 sales qualification questions (current situation, desired outcome, biggest obstacle, preferred solution type, open box)

Dynamic results page — three tiers:
- High-quality lead → 1:1 meeting offer
- Mid-tier lead → group event / webinar
- Low engagement → free content (podcast, guide, article)

Target conversion: 20–40% for well-matched audience and hook.

| Lever | Lift | Source type |
|---|---|---|
| Verbatim buyer language in headline | Highest single-change impact | Messaging research |

## Onboarding: Aha-Moment Framework

The aha moment = the first moment the user FEELS the product's value (not reads about it).

- Airbnb: first booking
- Netflix: finding and watching a show
- Assessment tool: seeing your score and personalized recommendations

Every onboarding screen decision must be evaluated against: "Does this move the user closer to the aha moment?"

Key patterns that increase aha-moment reach:
- Show product in action on welcome screen — not a feature list
- Personalization questions that visibly change what user sees next
- Show what answers unlocked ("Your plan is ready" — not "Thank you for answering")
- Outcome framing with specificity: "In 60 days you'll be able to [X]" — not "Improve your X"
- Sell outcomes on every screen, not features
