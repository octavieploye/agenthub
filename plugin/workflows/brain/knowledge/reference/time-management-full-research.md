## RESEARCH AREA 1 — MANAGER VS MAKER SCHEDULE

### Paul Graham's Original Essay (2009)
Source: https://www.paulgraham.com/makersschedule.html

- Two types of schedule: manager's (1-hour slots, 20+/day) and maker's (half-day minimums)
- For a maker, a 1-hour meeting at 2pm costs 3-4 hours: the hour before, the meeting, the hour after
- Meetings are a disaster for makers — they can't get into flow knowing they'll be pulled out

### Hybrid Solutions
- **Office hours model:** Cluster all meetings at the end of the workday (Graham's approach)
- **Partitioned days/weeks:** Graham coded dinner-to-3am, then business stuff 11am-to-dinner
- **Meeting placement:** If a meeting involves a maker, schedule at very beginning or very end of day

### Hormozi's Evolution Model
Source: Video transcription (Alex Hormozi)

- **V1:** Maker time during nights, weekends, early mornings (starting out, no calendar control)
- **V2:** 50/50 split — first half maker, second half manager. Plan day back-to-front from 5pm.
- **V3:** Entire Maker Days (2-4/week) + stacked Manager Days (1-2/week)
- A 30-min meeting in a morning block doesn't cost 30 min — it destroys the entire block
- The maker's dilemma: say no = offend the manager; say yes = destroy the morning

### Zeigarnik Effect
- Open loops (scheduled meetings) consume cognitive bandwidth even before they happen
- You set timers, start mentally preparing, can't enter deep flow
- A future meeting destroys current maker time

## RESEARCH AREA 2 — TIME BLOCKING & CALENDAR ARCHITECTURE

### Core Methods
- **Time blocking:** Assign every hour a purpose — no unassigned slots
- **Time boxing:** Fixed duration per task (Parkinson's Law: work expands to fill time available)
- **Day theming:** Monday = big-picture/growth, Tuesday = development, Wednesday = content, etc.
- **Micro-blocking:** 10-20 min bursts for small tasks, batched together, not sprinkled through the day

### Elite Practitioner Patterns

**Elon Musk — 5-Minute Blocks**
- Entire day planned in 5-minute increments
- Each block is a "world" — full entry and exit
- Batches similar cognitive tasks to reduce switching cost
- Volume handled through density of time use, not length of day

**Bill Gates — Think Weeks**
- Twice/year, 7-day solo retreat — reading and thinking only
- No meetings, no calls, sometimes 18h/day of reading
- Produced the 1995 "Internet Tidal Wave" memo that redirected Microsoft
- Principle: deep thinking and operations cannot share time blocks — must be structurally separated

**Jeff Bezos — Morning Protection**
- No meetings before 10am
- Max 3 high-stakes decisions per day
- "My job is to make a small number of high-quality decisions"

**Steve Jobs — Focus Through Elimination**
- Radically reduced decision surface by eliminating meetings, products, relationships
- The 4-product matrix was a time architecture decision, not just a product decision

### Back-to-Front Planning
- Plan from 5pm backward to keep mornings open
- Fixed commitments go in first, work fills around them
- Prevents morning blocks from being colonized by meetings

### Solopreneur-Specific
- Standard calendars only show meetings, not work — this is the core problem
- Block first 2-3 hours for deep work before opening inbox
- Start with whatever moves revenue: writing, building, client deliverables, strategy
- Reactive work (email, messages, admin) gets afternoon hours

## RESEARCH AREA 3 — ENERGY MANAGEMENT

### Loehr & Schwartz — The Power of Full Engagement (2003)
Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17972496/

Core argument: Energy, not time, is the fundamental currency of high performance.
Time is fixed. Energy is renewable and manageable.

**Four energy dimensions (all must be managed):**
1. Physical — sleep, nutrition, movement, hydration
2. Emotional — positive emotions, self-regulation, resilience
3. Mental — focus, creativity, concentration, realistic optimism
4. Spiritual — purpose, values alignment, commitment beyond self-interest

**Key critique:** Conventional time management targets mental energy while ignoring
the physical and emotional energy that makes mental energy possible.

### Ultradian Rhythms
Source: https://www.asianefficiency.com/productivity/ultradian-rhythms/

- 90-minute high-performance cycles followed by 20-minute renewal periods
- Heart rate, hormonal levels, muscle tension, brainwave activity all cycle together
- Working through the renewal period degrades performance in the next cycle
- Elite athletes spend 5x more time recovering than average athletes — same pattern in knowledge work

### Key Principles
- Top performers oscillate: full engagement followed by deliberate recovery
- Rituals (not willpower) sustain high performance
- Stress is not the enemy — insufficient recovery is the enemy
- Build recovery into the calendar as hard blocks, not optional fillers
- Protect physical energy as a prerequisite to cognitive performance, not a reward for it

## RESEARCH AREA 4 — CONTEXT SWITCHING COST

### Harvard Business Review (2022)
- Knowledge workers toggle between applications/websites 1,200 times per day
- ~4 hours per week spent merely reorienting after switches
- Over a year: ~5 working weeks (~9% of annual work time) lost to context switching

### Porter & Nohria CEO Study (Harvard Business School, 2018)
Source: https://hbr.org/2018/07/how-ceos-manage-time

- Tracked 27 CEOs across 60,000 hours over 13 weeks (in 15-minute increments)
- CEOs averaged 9.7h/weekday, 3.9h weekends, 2.4h vacation = ~62.5h/week
- 61% of work time in face-to-face meetings, only 24% alone
- Proactive calendar ownership was the single biggest differentiator between
  high and low CEO effectiveness
- Strategy and long-range thinking were the most commonly under-allocated category

## RESEARCH AREA 5 — DECISION FATIGUE

### Core Research
- Israeli parole board study: approval rates drop from ~65% at session start to
  near 0% before breaks, then reset after food/rest
- Knowledge workers make ~30,000 decisions per day — even trivial ones deplete reserves
- Decision quality peaks in the morning and degrades across the day

### Countermeasures
1. **Uniform strategy:** Obama — "I only wear grey or blue suits" — eliminate trivial decisions
2. **Front-load hard decisions:** First 2-3 hours of the day, before fatigue accumulates
3. **Decision batching:** Group similar decisions into single sessions
4. **Default rules:** "I always decline dinner meetings", "No calls before 9am" —
   eliminates entire classes of micro-decisions
5. **Standard routines:** Same wake time, go-to breakfast, standard outfits

## RESEARCH AREA 6 — PRIORITIZATION FRAMEWORKS

### Income Producing Activities (IPA)
- IPAs are tasks with direct revenue impact: lead gen, sales, customer retention, upsells
- Do IPAs first — everything else is secondary
- Score tasks 1-10 on revenue impact. Below 5 = delegate, automate, or delete.
- Your time is worth $200-$1,000/hr on high-impact work — don't waste it on low-value tasks

### Theory of Constraints (Hormozi)
- Every business has ONE primary factor limiting growth
- Ask: "What problem, if solved, makes all other problems pale?"
- Focus there. Postpone everything else.
- "What am I hoping to accomplish? Is this worth doing at all?"

### MoSCoW Allocation
- Must have: 50-60% of effort (non-negotiable, project fails without)
- Should have: 10-20% (high value, project survives without)
- Could have: ~20% (nice-to-have, first to cut)
- Won't have: 0% (explicitly not now)
- Must items should not exceed 60% — exceeding signals scope is too large

### Eisenhower Matrix
- Urgent+Important = do now
- Important+Not Urgent = schedule in maker blocks (this is where growth happens)
- Urgent+Not Important = delegate or batch
- Not Urgent+Not Important = delete

### Leverage Hierarchy
1. Revenue generation (keeps business alive)
2. System building (do once, benefit forever)
3. Customer acquisition (pipeline for future revenue)
4. Strategic decisions (wrong direction wastes everything)
5. Relationship building (compounds over time)
6. Admin/maintenance (necessary but never first)

### Dan Koe Focus Rule
- 4h focused > 12h scattered
- For side-builders: 1h focused weekday + 4h focused weekend
- Consistency compounds. Volume without focus does not.

### 3-3-3 Method (Oliver Burkeman)
- 3 hours deep work + 3 medium tasks + 3 small tasks per day
- Reduces overwhelm, creates structure, ensures steady progress
- Works well alongside Eisenhower for categorization

### Pareto Principle (80/20)
- 80% of results come from 20% of efforts
- Identify those high-impact tasks, eliminate or delegate the rest

## RESEARCH AREA 7 — WORKFLOW-TO-CALENDAR MAPPING

### Multi-Stage Deliverables
Every deliverable is a pipeline, not a single block.

**Video production stages:** development (idea+script) -> pre-production (storyboard,
casting, location) -> production (filming) -> post-production (editing, color, sound)
-> distribution (export, platform delivery, archiving)

**Podcast production stages:** planning (research, outline, guest prep) -> recording ->
editing -> show notes + assets -> publishing + promotion

**Content creation pipeline:** ideation -> drafting -> review -> revision -> publishing
-> distribution -> analytics review

### Time Estimation Rules
- Estimate each stage independently
- Add 25% buffer to total
- Gather actual durations vs planned to improve future estimates
- Map stages to different days based on energy type required

### Calendar Integration
- Kanban, Gantt, Timeline, or Calendar view — switch perspectives without rebuilding
- Template standard workflows: create once, reuse for each episode/project
- Calendar-style templates map recording, editing, publishing across weeks

## RESEARCH AREA 8 — BACKWARD PLANNING (WORKBACK SCHEDULE)

### Core Method
Start at the end goal, work through required steps in reverse-chronological order.

### Benefits
- Forces you to identify every phase and task required
- Immediately reveals whether targets are realistic
- Allocates specific resources (time, people, tools) to each stage
- Prevents the optimism bias of forward planning

### Buffer Rules
- Buffer is non-negotiable — if one phase overruns, it steals from the next
- The deadline is immovable; everything else adjusts
- Assign deadlines to each task to identify unrealistic targets early

### Resource Allocation
- Schedule specific expertise for specific dates (scarce resources first)
- Identify bottleneck resources and schedule them before anything else

## RESEARCH AREA 9 — TASK TRIAGE (HUMAN vs AI vs OUTSOURCE)

### The Shift (2025-2026)
- Solopreneurs building AI agent stacks instead of hiring human support
- Complete solopreneur AI stack: $3,000-$12,000/year vs $36,000-$60,000/year for a VA
- AI handles: email triage, calendar management, bookkeeping, content drafting, research

### Delegation Framework
- Prioritize: what only YOU can do vs what can be systematized
- AI-first for non-revenue-generating work: admin, drafts, research, data
- Outsource when human calendar is full and AI cannot handle the task
- Human retains: strategic decisions, relationship calls, live presentations, final approval

### Human-AI Co-Creation Patterns
Research identifies 5 collaboration modes:

1. **Hands-off:** Human assigns, AI delivers autonomously (no human time)
2. **Observational:** AI works, human monitors and intervenes if needed (minimal time)
3. **Directive:** Human sets strategy, AI executes sub-tasks (split time)
4. **Concurrent:** Human and AI work simultaneously on same deliverable (full time, 2-5x output)
5. **Iterative:** AI drafts -> human reviews -> AI revises -> human finalizes (spread across sessions)

### Key Insight
Complex and interdependent creative tasks thrive in human-AI co-creation.
Repetitive and rule-based tasks should be fully delegated to AI.
The human stays in the decision-making loop for effectiveness.

## RESEARCH AREA 10 — BOUNDARIES & ASYNC COMMUNICATION

### Async-First Test
Before scheduling any meeting, ask: could this be an email, recorded video, or
shared document with comments? If yes, do not schedule the meeting.

### Communication Batching
- Designated times for email/Slack — not reactive throughout the day
- Theme communication by type: client responses at 11am, team updates at 3pm

### Protecting Focus Time
- "Office hours" model for handling multiple stakeholders
- Tell people when you'll be slow to respond — people get upset at unmet
  expectations, not delayed replies

### Shutdown Ritual
- Review all inboxes, notes, and communications at end of day
- Ensure every incomplete task is recorded in a trusted system
- Creates psychological dividing line between work and personal time
- Prevents rumination on unfinished work during personal time

### Weekly Review (GTD)
David Allen calls it "the critical success factor" for the entire system.
- 20-60 minute ritual: stop, clear everything off your mind, review where things
  stand, plan the week ahead
- A task list you do not review stops being trusted
- A list you do not trust is one you ignore

### Mandated Quiet Time
- Team-wide no-meeting, no-Slack blocks
- Example: "Wednesday is quiet day for editors"
- Applies especially to makers: engineers, writers, developers, designers
