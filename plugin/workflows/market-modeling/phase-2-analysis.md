# Phase 2 — Five-Lens Analysis
Role: Analyst
Receives: HARVEST-OUTPUT from Phase 1
Gate output: ANALYSIS-OUTPUT → Shadow (Phase 3)

---

## OBJECTIVE

Apply five model-family lenses to the harvested intelligence. Do not choose the best lens. Do not resolve the tension between them. The tension is the signal. Map what each lens sees and what each structurally cannot see.

---

## LENS 1 — EQUILIBRIUM (PE/CGE)

What supply-demand dynamics structure this market?
- What elasticities are material — own-price, cross-price, income?
- Where does partial equilibrium fail (cross-market effects significant enough to require GE)?
- What does a general equilibrium view add that single-market analysis misses?
- What does this lens assume — rational agents, competitive markets, no externalities?
- What does this lens structurally cannot generate?

---

## LENS 2 — DYNAMIC (DSGE/VAR)

What time-series dynamics and shock transmission are relevant?
- What shocks propagate through this market and over what horizon?
- What impulse responses matter for this question?
- What does DSGE or VAR modeling of similar markets historically reveal?
- What does this lens assume — equilibrium, rational expectations, representative agent?
- What does this lens structurally cannot generate?

---

## LENS 3 — AGENT-BASED / COMPLEXITY

What emergent phenomena are visible or likely in this market?
- Lock-in effects? Network externalities? Path dependence? QWERTY dynamics?
- Herding behavior? Volatility clustering? Boom-bust cycles?
- What would an ABM reveal that equilibrium analysis assumes away?
- What stylized facts must a credible model of this market reproduce?
- What does complexity economics reveal about who wins — efficiency or first-mover advantage?

---

## LENS 4 — POST-KEYNESIAN / MINSKY

What is the financial fragility structure of this market?
- Minsky position: are actors in hedge, speculative, or Ponzi financing states?
- Degree of monopoly: who sets prices by markup? What does that mean for income distribution?
- Sectoral balance sheets: what do assets and liabilities across sectors reveal?
- Endogenous money: how does credit creation shape dynamics in this market?
- Kalecki's insight: is income distribution here determined by power or productivity?

---

## LENS 5 — ECOLOGICAL / TRUE COST

What externalities does the market price hide?
- What natural capital is being depleted, and is it being booked as income or loss?
- What planetary boundary indicators are relevant to this market's activity?
- What does ISEW/GPI accounting reveal that GDP accounting masks?
- What is the throughput cost — what flows from nature through this market and back as waste?
- Who pays what the market price does not carry?

## WHAT THIS PHASE DOES NOT DO

- Does not declare one lens correct and the others peripheral
- Does not skip a lens because the harvest did not directly address it (silence is a finding — name it)
- Does not attempt synthesis — that is Phase 4
- Does not attempt a conclusion — the Analyst produces material, the Synthesiser produces a picture
